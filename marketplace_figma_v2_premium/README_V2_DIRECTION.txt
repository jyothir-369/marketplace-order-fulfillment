MARKETPLACE — V2 PREMIUM DIRECTION (redo, in response to "too wireframe / not stakeholder-ready")

WHAT CHANGED FROM V1

v1 was structurally complete but visually a wireframe: flat gray boxes, a single
weight of black text, hexagon-icon photo placeholders, no brand point of view.
v2 is a full re-key of the visual system:

  Palette   Ink navy (#16233f) + brass/gold accent (#a9803f) on warm ivory
            (#faf7f1), replacing the flat black/white/gray v1 palette.
  Type      Serif display (Playfair Display, falls back to Georgia) for
            headlines and brand moments; Inter stays for body/UI/operational
            density. v1 was Inter everywhere, which read generic.
  Imagery   Soft gradient "photography" blocks standing in for real product
            photos, instead of flat #f4f4f5 boxes with a hexagon glyph.
  Elevation Larger, softer shadows; hairline borders in warm gray instead of
            cool gray; gold rule accents on active nav/section headers.
  Voice     Storefront copy is editorial ("Shop from vendors you can trust"),
            not just functional labels.

WHY ONLY THESE FIVE, NOT ALL 12 FROM v1

These are the screens a stakeholder or client actually looks at to judge
"does this feel premium" — brand cover, the storefront a buyer opens on,
the catalog, a product page, and the operator's command center. Rather than
re-skin all 12 v1 boards at variable quality, this set proves the direction
at full quality on the highest-visibility screens first.

If this direction is approved, the same tokens (palette, serif/sans pairing,
photo-block treatment, navy+gold operational chrome) extend directly onto
the remaining v1 boards (checkout, cart, vendor directory, vendor portal
operations, admin sub-screens, dialogs/states) — same layout logic as v1,
new skin. Say the word and I'll carry it through the rest of the set.

STILL A CAIRO/SANDBOX RENDERING NOTE

Playfair Display isn't installed in this sandbox's SVG renderer, so the
PNG previews show a bold sans-serif fallback for headlines instead of the
actual serif. Figma (and any real browser) has Playfair Display available
and will render it correctly on import — the SVG font-family declarations
are already correct for that.
